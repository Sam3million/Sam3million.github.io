This was my team's Senior Design project at UCF. Monoball is a single rotor, spherically enclosed,
ducted camera drone meant for reconaissance and search-and-rescue operations. The team was composed
of 4 Aerospace Engineering majors (including myself), and 2 Computer Engineering majors.

# Background

The Monoball project began in Fall 2025 as a UCF Senior Design project sponsored by Lockheed Martin.
The project was pitched as a reconaissance and search-and-rescue drone. The main use case is
entering environments where it would be dangerous for humans to go. For example, a collapsed building
or wildfire. The protective outer shell allows the drone to bump into walls and obstacles
in these environments. It transmits a live video feed back to the operator so they can
navigate and explore. The sponsor also requested that it have a rolling mode, where
the drone could roll on the ground instead of flying. This theoretically saves battery life
and allows for traversal in height-constrained environments.

# Requirements

The team met with the sponsor to generate a list of requirements. Some of those included:
- 30 minute flight time
- Beach ball sized or smaller
- Roll mode
- High quality camera feed
- Ducted propeller
- GPS

# Design

We came up with initial design concepts based on our background research.

![Initial Design](/assets/monoball/initial-design.png)
*Initial Design*

The main issues with this design were weight and mounting space.
The thick PLA construction of the internal components and the outer shell 
would have been too heavy for a single motor to lift. There also wasn't enough mounting space
for all the internal components 

## Revision 2

The second design was inspired by a research paper that built a similar monocopter drone.
It uses modular plates that can be mounted on a series
of carbon fiber tubes. Small flanges and walls were later added to the top plate to provide more
mounting area.

![New Design](/assets/monoball/new-design.png)
*New Design*

# Assembly

The assembly process was difficult. We faced many issues due to factors such as
insufficient tolerances, material shrinkage, and broken components. Lots of sanding was required
to get everything clearing and fitting properly. The internal components also ended up being
more cluttered than expected.

# Flight Test

We were unable to achieve liftoff of the  drone. Theoretically, the single motor
was strong enough to lift the drone's weight. However, due to instability of the system,
it would start oscillating or rolling instead of taking off. There were also software
difficulties with ArduPilot and getting full throttle out of the motor.

# Camera Feed

Our camera feed used a HereLink transmitter and integrated well with the Cube Orange
and ArduPilot. The transmission quality was great.

# Final Thoughts

This was a great project that I definitely learned a lot from. If I were to redo it from
scratch the top three improvements I would make are choosing a much smaller battery,
developing a custom PCB to compactify electronics, and increasing the area of the control surfaces.
